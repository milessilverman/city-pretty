BACKGROUND

AHSC Program

The AHSC Program is a competitive state funding program with the primary objective to reduce
GHG emissions by providing funding for affordable housing development, transportation related
improvements, and programs that encourage residents to walk, bike, and use transit. Funded by
the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund (GGRF) auction proceeds from California’s Cap-and-Trade
program, AHSC funding creates holistic communities with affordable housing and transportation
options near jobs and other key destinations. Established in 2014, AHSC is administered by the
Strategic Growth Council and implemented by the California Department of Housing and
Community Development (HCD). The AHSC Program receives an annual statutory allocation (per
SB 862) of 20 percent of the total GGRF auction proceeds through 2030. To date, the AHSC
Program has awarded over $1.6 billion in grant funds for projects across the state.

Successful projects are defined as those that substantially reduce GHG emissions by increasing
access to affordable housing, employment centers, and key destinations through sustainable
transportation options. As a result, projects include both an affordable housing component and a
sustainable transportation scope of work. Projects are competitively scored based on established
scoring criteria, including GHG emissions reductions efficiency, length of active transportation
improvements, and green building status. In Round 6, the maximum funding request per project
was $30 million to fund both the affordable housing development and the transportation-related
improvements. Funding comes in the form of a loan for the affordable housing project and a grant
for the transportation infrastructure component on a reimbursement basis.

Impact to the City of Los Angeles

On January 26, 2022, the SGC awarded the City of Los Angeles a total of $166.1 million for the
sixth round of AHSC to support seven major projects amounting to over 900 new housing units.
As a successful joint applicant with seven housing developers, the City will be responsible for the
administration, implementation, construction, and completion of the grant-funded transportation-
related improvements, and the developers will be responsible for the construction of affordable
housing projects and other housing related infrastructure. The SGC’s Award letters are available
in Attachment A.

Given the AHSC program’s commitment to increasing access to affordable housing and
sustainable transportation infrastructure, the City has the opportunity to further leverage and
maximize future AHSC funding to advance the City’s priorities related to housing, mobility and
sustainability. Combined with the prior five rounds of AHSC awards, the City has successfully
secured approximately $494.2 million to support a total of 35 housing developments amounting to
over 3,800 new housing units, along with an array of GHG emission-reducing transit infrastructure
projects. In Round 6 alone, an estimated $54.8 million in grant funding will be implemented and
invested in Los Angeles for sustainable transportation infrastructure related improvements,
including 7.23 miles of pedestrian and cycling improvements. These improvements were
developed in alignment with the Mayor’s Green New Deal, Mobility Plan 2035, Safe Route to
Schools, Measure M Local Return Program, as well as the Vision Zero initiative to enhance
mobility, safety, and sustainability and through the concerted efforts of the City’s AHSC Team.
                                                                                                 3
The combined investment in housing and infrastructure will continue to contribute to a Los
Angeles that is more livable and more environmentally sustainable. For Round 6, the total GHG
reduction for all seven projects is projected to be 252,822 metric tons. This GHG total is equivalent
to GHG emissions from 54,984 passenger vehicles driven for one year or from 30,446 homes’
energy use for one year.

AHSC Round 6 Application Process and Awards

As in prior AHSC funding cycles, the City of Los Angeles held its Developers’ Kick-Off Meeting
on July 15, 2020. Due to the Covid-19 pandemic, the meeting was held virtually via Zoom and
attended by 123 developers and stakeholders. The City’s AHSC team consists of LAHD, LADOT,
BOE, BSL, BSS and DCP. The grant funding will be used for capital improvements, soft costs,
and administration costs related to the awarded transportation infrastructure projects. The City’s
AHSC team and other representatives provided presentations on key issues including an overview
of Round 6 threshold requirements, application process and timeline, impact and outcomes, and
project selection criteria. Developers were encouraged to submit a City of Los Angeles Call for
Projects form online, so the City could gather preliminary information about each potential project.
Through this process, the City received 24 application submissions. The City’s AHSC team then
proceeded to evaluate all potential projects based on the AHSC Round 6 threshold and the City’s
adopted criteria. Based on the review and approval by the AHSC City team, projects were invited
to attend one-on-one Developers’ Clinics that were held at City Hall and included participating
AHSC City departments, staff from various City Council District offices, affordable housing
developers, and technical consultants. During the Clinics, the City presented project elements,
application components, and potential transportation and infrastructure improvements.

At the conclusion of the process outlined above, the City co-applied for $184 million in AHSC
funding for eight projects, of which $119 million was requested for affordable housing
development, $61.7 million for transportation-related improvements, and $3.2 million for program
activities (CF No. 15-0087-S6). On January 26, 2022, the City was awarded seven out of its eight
project applications totaling $166.1 million in AHSC funding (see Table 1).

ACCEPTANCE OF AHSC GRANT FUNDING

Allocation of Funding to the City of Los Angeles

As outlined in the Joint Application Agreement with each developer, the City of Los Angeles is
the recipient of the grant funding related to Sustainable Transportation Improvements (STI) and
Transportation Related Amenities (TRA) (see Attachment B). The City will therefore be
responsible for completing the proposed scope of work while the developer concurrently
completes the affordable housing project. Both components must be completed within five years
of the award letter.

Capital, Soft and Administration Costs

As outlined in Table 2 below, of the $54,782,000 total grant award for STI and TRA, $26,621,894
is reserved for capital costs. Capital costs include those that are directly related to the construction
of the transportation project performed by the Contractor or City staff including labor, equipment
and material, while $6,680,118 is reserved for soft costs. Soft costs include those that are
incidentally, but not directly related to project plans, specifications, and estimates including, but
not limited to, planning, engineering, construction management, design work, and environmental
impact reports. Lastly, $3,158,988 is reserved for administrative costs. Administrative costs are
incurred by the public agencies which implement the transportation projects, including grant
administration, inspections, and reporting (For complete information on Round 6 budgets for
transportation improvements per project, please see Attachment C). The final application budgets
are reflected in Exhibit C.
