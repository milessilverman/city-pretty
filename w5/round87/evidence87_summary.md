## road_energy

checked: 25; page read 23, quote found 14, supports claim 23; not checked 4

Freeways: a new freeway usually lowers prices of the nearest homes, with losses fading by distance; access gains show up farther out and in commercial and multifamily property. A recent Phoenix study found −12% after announcement and −20% after opening within 2 miles (−26% within ¼ mile) versus homes 3–5 miles away; ramp access offset over half the loss. Older U.S. studies found abutting-home discounts of 0.5–16% (often about 5%) and access gains of 9–17% a few blocks out; land at interchanges "frequently soars". Regionally, interstate lane-km raise driving about one-for-one. Caps and removals: modeled neighborhood amenities are 18% lower beside freeways, fading by about 2.4 miles; San Francisco removals raised nearby prices about 7–13% (secondhand); caps cost about $320–667M per mile and pass a benefit test mainly in central areas. Transmission lines: the sign is usually negative but small and local, about 0–10%, typically 3–6% within a few hundred feet, shrinking with distance and time. New UK pylons cut prices 3.6% within 1.2 km with no pre-construction drop; in Salt Lake County, 138 kV lines cost 5% within 50 m while 345 kV greenway corridors showed no loss. Benefits: planners report benefit-cost ratios of 1.6–3.5; LBNL finds many links worth over $130M per GW-year. MISO costs: $3.2–4.1M/mile at 345 kV, $5.2–6.3M at 765 kV. Co-location: federal and Minnesota rules now allow lines in highway right-of-way; burial in road or rail corridors avoids visual and value effects but nearly doubled Northern Pass's cost. No study measures co-location's property-value effect.

**ACS caveats, quoted from Census documents I opened.**
- The value is the owner's own estimate, not a sale price.
- 5-year figures "cannot be used to describe what is going on in any particular year in the period".
- Dollars are CPI-adjusted to the last year of the window, and each window uses that year's tract boundaries, so tracts change at 2010 and 2020. I confirmed the Census crosswalk files exist.
- The top value category is $1,000,000+ in the 2009-2015 definitions and $2,000,000+ from 2016.

**Methods sources (both use transaction data, not a published index).**
- **Gupta, Van Nieuwerburgh and Kontokosta, NBER WP 26789 (Second Avenue Subway).** A year-by-year difference-in-differences design. Prices near the line were about 5% higher during 2007-12 construction and 11.2% higher in 2013-19, both against 2003-06. "Much of the value gain occurs in anticipation of the subway opening." The repeat-sales subsample shows a smaller effect (2.8% vs 8.3%).
- **Mathur 2019, Mineta Transportation Institute report 19-11 (Warm Springs BART, Fremont, CA).** Homes within 2 miles are compared with homes 2-5 miles away across six phases, against a 2000-01 pre-announcement base. The premium appears from the 2007-09 construction start onward.
- **A caveat worth putting on the site** (Philadelphia Fed, Severen 2020): price rises before opening may reflect expected non-transit amenities.
