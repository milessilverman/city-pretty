*Revised 9 Nov 2026, 00:20: the pre-delivery audit's reviewers finished after the 20:15 delivery and confirmed all 113 findings; their amendments are applied (section 8). No data changed.*

Round 8.5 follows round 8.4, delivered on 8 Nov at 09:50. An independent audit of round 8.4's code and report confirmed 56 problems (`ERRATA-round8.4.md`). Round 8.5 fixes most of the code ones and re-runs the counties whose bus values or stations they change. Some fixes are partial or wait for round 8.6 (section 9): a stop served only by headway trips outside 07:00–09:00 still shows 0 buses an hour (finding 4); Broward, Miami-Dade, Palm Beach, Fresno and Madera keep their selection-17 records of Tri-Rail's copy 333 and Clovis Transit (findings 2 and 3); only the 108 counties on selections 18 and 19 list the feeds read with no stop in the county, and 74 counties lack the candidate counts (findings 11 and 15); Jefferson, CO still measures Home James on 4 Nov, though the feed has no stop in or near the county (finding 1); and rail15_sim.py is fixed only in part (findings 12 and 13). It also counts buses at stops that timetables list without a time, merges duplicate station dots, and drops the stations no timetable confirms where OpenStreetMap marks them not open or the operator's current schedule omits the line (the JTA Skyway). It builds batch 6, the next 150 largest counties. The national site now has 639 counties: 637 pipeline counties plus LA and Orange, in the 48 contiguous states, DC and Puerto Rico. They hold 269.0 million people (ACS 2020–2024): 268.1 million of the 334.9 million in the 50 states and DC (80.0%), and 0.9 million of Puerto Rico's 3.2 million.

## 1. Buses at stops listed without a time (transit selection 17)
**The problem.** Many operators publish a time only at a trip's timepoints and leave the stops in between blank, which GTFS allows. The transit step skipped every blank stop time ("non-timepoint stop: no time to count"), so those stops counted no buses. A scan at 13:23 of every feed in the counties' transit metas (`untimed_scan.py` → `logs/untimed_scan.json`) found blank bus stop times in 126 of 594 feeds. The metas now count 596. Home James's feed, counted in Adams, Denver and Larimer, CO since their selection-17 re-runs, also leaves times blank; CamTran's does not. LYNX (Orlando, MobilityData 347) had the most: 321,819 of its 359,274 bus stop times (90%) are blank, and 3,423 of its 3,687 stops never have a time. On round 8.4's site, Orange County, FL had buses in 12 of its 673 block groups, and 568 showed 0 buses an hour. The audit found the same in Bayamón (finding 39).

**The fix.** Selection 17 interpolates each blank time between the trip's timed stops before counting the 07:00–09:00 departures. It uses distance along the route (`shape_dist_traveled`) when every stop of the trip has it, and stop order otherwise. Each feed's meta records how many stop times were interpolated (`untimed_stop_times_interpolated`).

**Effect.** The 209 counties served by a feed with blank times were re-run, together with Guilford, NC and Lucas, OH for audit findings 11 and 14 (`rebundle85b`, 211 of 211 exit 0). 173 of the 211 have a round-8.4 bundle, and 65 of those changed (`transit7_effect.py site84 logs/rebundle85b.log`):
- bus frequency changed in 1,036 block groups. All went up except one in Lucas, whose feed copy had been updated;
- 352 block groups that showed unknown now have a value;
- 194 now show unknown: San Juan's 108 and Bayamón's 86, under the Puerto Rico rule of section 4;
- bus-dependent projects changed in 337 block groups. In 71 of them, Puerto Rico block groups that became unknown under that rule lost their microtransit row; one is in Westchester, where a removed duplicate station dot lengthened the rail distance (section 2). The top-ranked project changed in 4 block groups (in 7 at some evidence tier the page offers, one of them Westchester's).

## 2. Duplicate station dots merged (metrics_oc v4 and v5)
A GTFS rail stop with no mapped station near it is added to the map (round 8.4, section 1). Round 8.4 merged such a stop with a mapped or already-added station only within 150 m, and 112 added stops lay 150–300 m from a mapped station. Examples: Metro-North's Milford and Waterbury, Caltrain's Bayshore, and Amtrak's "Boston" at South Station, 154 m from the mapped station.

**The rule** (`metrics_oc._same_station`). An added stop is the same station as a kept or already-added one when either:
- it lies within 150 m, as before; or
- it lies within 300 m and:
  - the names match (token sets with abbreviations expanded and generic words such as "station" or "street" dropped, Jaccard ≥ 0.5: "Crown Hts-Utica Av" / "Crown Heights–Utica Avenue", "Perris - South" / "South Perris"); or
  - the stop is Amtrak's, whose feed names stops by city.

Two refinements followed from the audit (v5, finding 7):
- a possessive 's is no longer read as "south";
- two added stops of one feed more than 150 m apart are never merged, by name or as Amtrak's, because the rule treats them as two stations. This restores New York's 50 St and 7 Av (Kings), Bedford Park Blvd–Lehman College (in Westchester's layer) and Metra's Blue Island–Vermont (in Will's). The test is made only between added stops, not against a mapped OpenStreetMap station, which carries no feed. In San Diego, NCTD's feed lists seven Coaster stations twice, 165–307 m apart, and each pair ends as its one mapped station, which is right.

Distinct stations under other names stay, unless one is Amtrak's: Farragut West, 212 m from Farragut North; Walnut-Locust, 200 m from PATCO's 12-13th & Locust; and SEPTA's 13th St, 267 m from 11th Street, the next stop on the Market–Frankford Line. In Delaware and Montgomery counties' layers, whose OpenStreetMap extracts leave out 30th Street, the subway's "Drexel Station at 30th St" and the railroad's "Gray 30th St Station", 245 m apart, are two dots: they are two stations. Philadelphia's own layer has OpenStreetMap's two 30th Street stations, 166 m apart.

**Effect.** A simulation on every county with the round-8.4 files, using the rule in `merge85_rule_sim.py`, found 121 additions to remove in 58 counties, and 2 stops they had masked. Only its county list was saved (`logs/merge85_queue.txt`); the counts are in the 09:52 entry of `logs/rebundle84.notes`. One of the 121, Westchester's North White Plains, was not a round-8.4 addition. Suffolk County, MA was rebuilt first as the test (exit 0), and `rebundle85a.sh` re-ran the other 57 (57 of 57 exit 0). Against round 8.4's site, the layers lost 120 additions and gained 1. Of the 120, 4 were distinct stations that v5 then restored (Kings' 50 St and 7 Av, Westchester's Bedford Park Blvd–Lehman College, Will's Blue Island–Vermont) and 2 were Amtrak's "Canadian Border" timing point (finding 6); most of the other 114 were duplicates. The one gained is SEPTA Regional Rail's "Gray 30th St Station" in Montgomery County's layer: it replaces Amtrak's "Philadelphia" and NJ Transit's "30TH ST. PHL." there. In Suffolk, Amtrak's "Boston" is gone; the county's two remaining additions, Hersey (Needham) and Swampscott, are MBTA stations 2 and 4 km outside the county, beyond its OpenStreetMap extract. At least three distinct stations are still merged: Amtrak's "Seattle" (King Street Station, section 9); in the Bronx's layer, LIRR's Flushing Main Street, 219 m from the subway's Flushing–Main St; and in Contra Costa's layer, Amtrak's Oakland Coliseum, 155 m from BART's Coliseum.

Round 8.5's county re-runs were compared with round 8.4's site (`station_effect.py site84`): 300 of them have a round-8.4 bundle (Orange's rebuild is in section 3). No rail distance got shorter. 1,700 block groups in 39 counties got longer, 470 of them in Duval, where the Skyway is dropped (section 3). Elsewhere the increase is at most 298 m, because a dot 150–300 m from the remaining station is gone (median 185 m in Lafayette Parish, LA; 190 m in Jackson County, MO). Mostly it is a duplicate; in Niagara (2 block groups, at most 30 m farther) it is the Canadian Border point. In those 300 counties, rail-dependent projects changed in 10 block groups and transit upzoning was removed in 6. The top-ranked project changed in 6 (in 9 at some evidence tier); these counts include the counties' bus changes.

## 3. Stations that are not open, and lines that are not running
**Stations OpenStreetMap marks as not open (metrics_oc v7).** A station tagged under construction, proposed, disused or abandoned is now kept only where a GTFS rail stop confirms it, never by type or operator. The audit found the case (finding 23): Orange, CA kept the OC Streetcar's Fairview, which OpenStreetMap tags as under construction (`construction=light_rail`) and OCTA's current GTFS does not serve. It was the nearest "rail station" for 460 of Orange's 2,049 block groups.

The 9 counties with stations kept by type or operator, and Orange, were re-run. The changes:
- **Orange:** Fairview and a duplicate Buena Park dot are gone, so its station layer goes from 20 to 18. 570 block groups are now farther from rail (median 2,369 m more) and Santa Ana's 3 transit-upzoning rows are removed. No top-ranked project changed.
- **Duval, FL:** seven Skyway stations that OpenStreetMap marks disused are dropped. Their last edit is dated 30 Oct 2026 in OpenStreetMap's live data, which the station-status check read (below). 404 block groups are farther from rail (median 1,882 m more).

**The Skyway's last station (metrics_oc v8).** OpenStreetMap left one Skyway station node without the disused tag: the Jacksonville Regional Transportation Center (last edited 21 Jan 2022); its two stop positions and the Skyway's track are tagged disused. v8 adds a list of lines whose operator's current schedule omits them (`pipeline/stations_not_served.json`, with the evidence). Their stations are not kept by type or operator; a GTFS rail stop within 400 m still keeps one, even a stop from an old copy (JTA's 2023 copy, which the transit step opens only if the current one cannot be used, lists all 8 Skyway stops and would bring the 8 stations back). The one entry is the JTA Skyway:
- JTA's GTFS in effect from 2 Nov 2026 (MobilityData copy, `feed_info.txt`: "Jacksonville Transportation Authority,https://www.jtafla.com,en,20261102,20270321,S1000222") has 40 routes, all buses.
- JTA's 2023 GTFS (MobilityData's legacy mirror) listed the line when it ran: "605,SKYWAY,"Skyway",,1" with the stop "JRTC at LaVilla", 9 m from OpenStreetMap's station.

The station-status check also found reports that JTA ended Skyway service early on 30 Oct and suspended it from 2 Nov in its budget cuts. This machine's network policy refuses those pages, so they are leads, not evidence. Duval was re-run with v8, and its one remaining station is Amtrak's Jacksonville stop. Against round 8.4 (both steps together), 470 of Duval's 582 block groups are farther from rail: the median increase is 7,022 m and the largest 8,772 m. One rail-dependent project, one transit-upzoning row and one top-ranked project changed. The county's `rail_confirm.json` records each Skyway station as left out, with `not_served: jta-skyway`.

**Lines kept by type, checked.** Stations kept by type are those no timetable confirms. The station-status check sent one researcher to each line kept that way (`logs/station_status85.json`). The operators' sites and the news sites are refused by this machine's network policy, so every finding on a line's status is a search-result excerpt. The pages read here are OpenStreetMap's live data for the Skyway, JTA's GTFS (read for v8) and the state's Cal-ITP tables on data.ca.gov, which list SacRT's feed but none of its stops and so settle nothing. Under the sourcing rule (a claim counts only when its source page was opened here) those are leads: nothing changes on them, and each is written down for a machine that can open the page. The planned refutation stage was stopped after its first reviewer, who could open none of the pages either.
- **JTA Skyway:** not running; dropped on JTA's own GTFS (above).
- **Las Colinas APT (Dallas County: 600 Las Colinas Boulevard, Urban Towers, Bell Tower, Tower 909):** the leads say the people mover has carried no one since 29 Aug 2020. Search excerpts of its operator's site read "THE APT SYSTEM IS CLOSED INDEFINITELY" (dcurd.org, not opened here), and an NCTCOG plan says its operation is halted indefinitely; an undated NCTCOG page says "two people mover systems currently operate in North Central Texas". MobilityData's catalogs list no feed for the line and the pages are refused here, so its stations stay. Dallas's map has four of them: three kept by type, and Tower 909, which DART's Las Colinas Urban Center stop, 180 m away, confirms. It is item 5 of round 8.6's list (`NEXT_ROUND.md`).
- **SacRT's Dos Rios and 7th & Railyards:** SacRT's releases in search results say Dos Rios opened to Blue Line passengers on 28 Sep 2026, and that 7th & Railyards was set to open on the Green Line on 15 Oct 2026; a SacRT release titled "SacRT Celebrates Grand Opening of New 7th & Railyards Station" reports its opening. Both are kept, so the map agrees with the leads.
- **Buffalo's Metro Rail:** NFTA's notices in search results say it runs on one track in two zones from 1 Nov 2026, with DL&W temporarily closed until mid or late November. All 14 of its stations are on the map: 12 are kept by type, and Canalside and Seneca count because Amtrak's Buffalo–Exchange Street stop is 273 m and 353 m away. DL&W, temporarily closed, stays on the map.
- **Las Vegas Monorail:** the operator's pages in search results show extended hours for the SEMA Show (3–6 Nov 2026) and modified-hours notices to 15 Dec 2026, and no closure date. Its 7 stations are kept.
- **Tren Urbano:** news in search results says all 16 stations have run normally since 28 Mar 2026, after LUMA's electrical work had closed Bayamón and Deportivo from 25 Mar, and that service is still free. Its stations are kept.

## 5. Weekday selection fixes (transit selections 17–19) and the Veterans Day re-run
From the audit's confirmed findings (`ERRATA-round8.4.md`, findings 1–4, 6, 11 and 14; the docstrings of finding 5 were corrected too):
- **Zero is no level** (finding 1): two Wednesdays without departures no longer outvote a real schedule. One built feed had this case: Home James's Winter Park Shuttle (MobilityData 607). At selection 16, Adams, Denver and Jefferson, CO measured it on 4 Nov, when it runs no trips 07:00–09:00, instead of 25 Nov (2 departures). The selection-17 re-runs of Adams and Denver measure 25 Nov and add its Denver Airport stop; no bus value changed. Jefferson's search box (the county's extent plus a margin, where GTFS feeds and stops are read) holds none of its stops. Larimer's round-8.4 build (selection 9) measured it on 11 Nov; its selection-17 re-run measures 25 Nov (5 stops, all in Grand Lake, outside the county), and no bus value changed.
- **"No bus service near today"** (finding 4) is judged on bus services, not trains or ferries, and the feed's other copies are then tried.
- **Holiday swaps** (finding 3, selection 18) no longer extend a schedule's end. MobilityData's Tri-Rail copy (333) lists holiday swaps to 25 Nov 2027, but its timetable ended on 12 Aug 2025. In a test run at selection 18 the copy is stale and Broward's 5,737 stops are identical; its legacy-mirror copy, tried since selection 17, has no bus routes, so the search stops there and Tri-Rail's own URL is not tried. The airport shuttle still runs and is counted from Tri-Rail's other feed. Broward, Miami-Dade and Palm Beach were built at selection 17, so their pages still list copy 333 as having no bus service near today; round 8.6 re-runs them.
- **Headway trips** (finding 2, selection 18): trips defined in `frequencies.txt` count in the weekday comparison. In a scratch run of Fresno at selection 18, Clovis Transit records 215 departures per Wednesday instead of 0, and no chosen day changes. Fresno's and Madera's delivered metas (selection 17) still record 0; round 8.6 re-runs both (`logs/rebundle86_queue.txt`).
- **Amtrak's "Canadian Border"** timing point is left out of the rail stops (finding 6).
- **Records for audits** (findings 11 and 14): since 15:53 the transit meta lists the feeds read with service but no stop in the county (`read_no_stops_here`). The 108 counties built at selections 18 and 19 have the list; the 529 on selections 9–17 do not. The weekday audit reads the list (none of the 465 counties it replayed at selection 18 has one), uses the newest selection's rules, and queues a county whose feed's calendar dates have changed since the build.
- **Feeds MobilityData marks "future"** (selection 19, found in the batch-6 review): the catalog step left out rows that only MobilityData's current catalog lists and that it marks "future" (rows also in the legacy catalog keep their legacy status and were read). That status dates from MobilityData's last processing of the feed. CamTran's feed (Johnstown, tld-4671) is marked "future", but its own calendar began on 6 Nov 2026 (`feed_info.txt`: 20261106 to 20290131, 22 bus routes). So Cambria County showed buses in 2 of its 128 block groups. Such rows are now candidates, and the step judges their calendars like any other feed's: a copy that ended over a year ago is stale, and a copy whose service starts after the build is measured on its own first Wednesdays, as if it were running (CamTran's began on 6 Nov). Only three US rows are affected. Norwalk Transit matters only to LA and Orange, which use their own feeds, and Adirondack Trailways' copy is left out anyway as multi-state (its box covers over 20 square degrees). Cambria, Blair and Westmoreland, whose search boxes overlap CamTran's, were re-run (`rebundle85h`, 3 of 3 exit 0). CamTran's 394 stops in Cambria's search box (382 in the county, 10 in and around Windber in Somerset County, and 2 in the Altoona area) are measured on 18 Nov: its calendar starts on 6 Nov, and 11 Nov is a holiday. Cambria now shows buses in 43 of its 128 block groups, against 2 before. Westmoreland's search box holds 40 CamTran stops, all in Cambria, and its bus values are unchanged against round 8.4. Blair's holds 8: 6 in Cambria and 2 in the Altoona area. One of them, IU8 Altoona, gives one Altoona block group 0.5 buses an hour, where Blair's batch-6 build showed 0.

**The weekday audit at selection 18** (`transit_day_audit.py`, run with selection 18 as the newest from 17:01 to 17:05; the script now names selection 19, which chooses days as 18 does) replayed every county built before selection 18 that records a feed: 465 counties, 1,623 county feeds. Every recorded day reproduces, and no recorded feed needs a re-run for the weekday choice. None of these counties' metas lists the feeds read with no stop in the county, so the audit cannot check those.

**The Veterans Day re-run.** Before selection 12, a county built on 8 Nov measured some feeds on Wednesday 11 Nov, Veterans Day. The audit showed those values match a normal Wednesday for the feeds that are recorded. A feed with no service in the county on the holiday, however, is in no record (the audit's Guilford and Kern cases), because older builds did not list feeds read with no stop. The 96 counties still measuring a feed on 11 Nov were re-run at selection 18, with Niagara, NY (`rebundle85e`, 97 of 97 exit 0). In those counties, the 154 county feeds (111 feeds) round 8.4 measured on 11 Nov are now measured on 4 or 18 Nov. No feed is newly counted or dropped, and bus frequency is unchanged in every block group (`vet85_effect.py`, `transit7_effect.py`: no bus-dependent or top-ranked project changed). So the Veterans Day measurements matched normal days, as the weekday audit had predicted, and no feed was hidden.

## 6. Batch 6: 150 more counties
Batch 6 is the 150 largest counties not built before among those with an OpenStreetMap file (`make_queue.py`, ACS 2020–2024 population). It runs from Jackson County, MS (145,249) down to Floyd County, GA (99,693): 17.8 million people in 41 states and Puerto Rico, including Ponce (132,760) and Caguas (125,586). Alaska and Hawaii have no OpenStreetMap file here, so Honolulu, Anchorage, Hawaii County, Maui and Matanuska-Susitna, all larger than Floyd County, are left out.

All 150 were built through the whole pipeline with their site suggestions, from 09:55 to 12:57. The workers' last county finished at 12:51 (`logs/batch6a.log`), and the last site re-run ended at 12:57 (`logs/build_18035.log`). Two had failed in the site step's charger download (AFDC): for Cowlitz, WA it answered the count request without a count, and for Delaware, IN it returned 4 stations after counting 3. `fetch_la.py` now retries the count and counts again when the pages disagree, and both site steps were re-run (12:55 and 12:57). The 38 batch-6 counties served by a feed with untimed stops were re-run with selection 17 (section 1).

The station decisions of all 150 batch-6 counties were read (`logs/station85_batch6_review.md` lists 43 of the 66 drops, the named ones tagged as trains, or with an operator or a network; the other 23 are only in the counties' `rail_confirm.json` files):
- 70 decisions are GTFS confirmations (65 distinct stations, since five sit in two neighbouring counties' layers), none is kept by type or operator, and 6 Amtrak stops are added;
- the 66 drops (59 distinct stations) are heritage and excursion lines (Reading Blue Mountain & Northern, Everett, Black River & Western, Black Hills Central, the Grand Canyon Railway, Verde Canyon and others), two funiculars no feed lists (the Johnstown Inclined Plane and Horseshoe Curve's), the CVG airport's people mover, park and miniature trains, freight and historic depots, and 10 unnamed OpenStreetMap points;
- they also include Northstar's Elk River and Big Lake in Sherburne, MN. Metro Transit's GTFS for 7 Nov–25 Dec 2026 has the Blue and Green lines but no Northstar route, so the stations are not served. Anoka County's Northstar stations were already dropped in round 8.4.

The review also found the CamTran gap (section 5). A new regional page, `city-pretty-us-batch-6.html`, shows eight of them: Coconino AZ (Flagstaff), Schuylkill PA, Rock Island IL, Hampton VA, Ponce PR, Missoula MT, Hunterdon NJ and Bossier LA.

## 9. Known limits
- **Bus schedules that have ended:** a feed copy whose bus schedule ended less than a year before the build is measured on its last Wednesday with morning departures, and no other copy or operator URL is then tried. 44 feeds in 109 counties are measured that way, on a Wednesday more than three weeks before the build, from copies up to 11 months old (each page's sources row gives the date measured). The largest: MARTA (Atlanta, 12 counties) on 19 Aug 2026, Metro St. Louis on 10 Jun, PSTA (Pinellas) on 3 Jun, Milwaukee County Transit on 19 Aug, SFMTA on 26 Aug, El Paso's Sun Metro on 10 Jun, Tucson's SunTran on 13 May, CDTA (Albany) on 26 Aug and NCTD on 29 Jul. For most of them this machine can reach no current copy: MobilityData marks 41 of the 44 feeds inactive, and the proxy refuses the operators' own URLs that were checked (MARTA's, SFMTA's and NCTD's lfportal.nctd.org). NCTD's hosted copy answers 403, so its legacy mirror is read and lfportal.nctd.org is not tried. Four more copies ended between 28 Oct and 1 Nov and are measured on 28 Oct under the three-week lapse rule: Fresno Area Express, Sonoma County Transit, BJCTA and Grays Harbor Transit, in 10 counties. Round 8.6 looks for current copies (`NEXT_ROUND.md` item 3).
