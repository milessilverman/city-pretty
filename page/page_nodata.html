Methodology & Dataset Vintages

City Pretty estimates how much home value and quality of life each public improvement would add in every Census block group of Los Angeles County, and what it would cost.

Return per $1, evidence grades and ranges

Each project's return is the home value it adds to the homes it reaches, divided by its full cost (construction, land and ten years of upkeep, discounted). The price effect is the low end of the best study found, scaled by the study's distance and the local home values; the field report's improvement library lists every study. The band shown with each estimate is a judgment band by evidence grade, not a statistical confidence interval: ±9% for grade A (measured on LA sales), ±12% for B (studied elsewhere), ±16% for C (transferred from a related study) and ±22% for D and U (assumptions and unverified leads). An experimental local adjustment for heat, canopy gap, bus frequency, dining density, freeway proximity and price tier appears inside each project's details for context; it does not change the ranking, the map or the headline. Street-tree capital cost uses the City of LA in-lieu fee of $1,945 per Public Works tree (LAMC § 62.177, Ord. 185,573; covers procurement, planting and three years of watering; the development-requirement fee is $2,612 and small residential lots pay $267) plus ongoing upkeep and mortality.

EIFD tax-increment financing

Enhanced Infrastructure Financing Districts (Gov. Code § 53398.50 and following) pledge part of the growth in the 1% property tax. The calculator's base is the net taxable value on the 2026 Assessor roll for every parcel in the area, and the city and County shares come from the Auditor-Controller's FY 2026-27 Modified ATI ratios (AF49 report) for the area's actual tax-rate areas, weighted by value: 13,392 tax-rate areas, 99.99% of net taxable value matched. School and ERAF shares (about 47% of the 1% levy county-wide, value-weighted) cannot be pledged. Project-level funding plans assume 3.5% of homes resell each year (Prop 13 reassesses only on sale), a 2% yearly roll increase, 30-year bonds at 5.25% and 1.35× debt-service coverage, with the city pledging 75% of its share and the County 49% (the Artesia district's terms).

Measure W (Safe Clean Water Program)

Stormwater and green-infrastructure co-funding follows the LA County Flood Control District's Safe Clean Water Program: 50% Regional Program (projects scored by Watershed Area Steering Committees on a 110-point scale, 60 points to qualify, with benefits to disadvantaged communities at least 110% of their share of the population), 40% Municipal Program paid directly to cities, 10% District Program.

Aerial imagery

The aerial layer is USGS The National Map "Imagery Only" service, a public-domain federal basemap that needs no account or key.
